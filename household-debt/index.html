Insight of the month · November 2026

Mortgage arrears are falling, but the cost of borrowing is rising

Homeowner mortgage arrears fell for the tenth quarter in a row, to 76,080. But borrowing is getting more expensive. The Bank of England raised Bank Rate to 4% on 5 November, the average rate on new mortgages rose to 4.63% in September from 4.22% in May, and credit cards and overdrafts cost over 20%.

Who is most affected: people whose fixed-rate mortgage deal is ending, as they move to higher rates. UK Finance expected 1.8 million fixed-rate mortgages to come to an end in 2026. And people relying on credit cards and overdrafts, where the average rate is 21.71% and 20.79%.

Sources: UK Finance, Q3 2026 and mortgage market forecasts (15 Dec 2025); Bank of England, Money and Credit, Sep 2026; Monetary Policy Summary, 5 Nov 2026.

Borrowing on cards and loans

People in the UK borrowed a net £1.9 billion of consumer credit in September 2026: £0.7 billion on credit cards and £1.2 billion through other credit such as car finance and personal loans. That was down from £2.7 billion in August.

Consumer credit was growing at 9.8% a year, up from 7.3% in September 2025. Credit cards grew fastest, at 13.1%.

What borrowing costs

The Bank of England raised Bank Rate to 4% on 5 November 2026, from 3.75%. The rates households actually pay are much higher for unsecured borrowing. In September an interest-charging credit card cost 21.71% on average and an overdraft 20.79%, against 9.85% for a new personal loan.

The average rate on newly drawn mortgages rose to 4.63% in September, from 4.22% in May. On all outstanding mortgages it was 4.03%. In its November report the Bank of England projects that average annual mortgage payments will be around £1,500 higher by the end of 2029 than before the conflict in the Middle East.

Mortgage arrears: still falling

76,080 homeowner mortgages were in arrears of at least 2.5% of the balance at the end of September 2026. That is 0.87% of all homeowner mortgages, 2% fewer than in the previous quarter and 10% fewer than a year earlier: the tenth quarterly fall in a row.

Lenders took 1,840 properties into possession in the quarter, 20% fewer than a year earlier. Possessions of owner-occupied homes were unchanged on the quarter, while buy-to-let possessions rose 10% to 690.

Insolvencies: rising

11,894 people in England and Wales entered insolvency in September 2026, 9% more than a year earlier. Over the past 12 months that is one adult in 357 (28.0 per 10,000), up from one in 403 a year before.

Most were individual voluntary arrangements (IVAs), a formal deal to repay part of what is owed. The 7,385 IVAs in September were the most in any month since November 2022, apart from December 2025, which was inflated by a backlog of cases.

Elsewhere in the UK, Scotland recorded 2,181 individual insolvencies in April to June 2026, 13% more than a year earlier, and Northern Ireland 189 in September 2026.

What it means for households

Borrowing on cards and loans is growing quickly, and it is expensive. At the average credit card rate of 21.71%, a £1,000 balance that isn't paid down costs roughly £217 a year in interest.

Most mortgage holders are keeping up with payments, and arrears are low. But new mortgages cost more than at the start of the year, and Bank Rate went up in November, so people coming off fixed-rate deals face higher payments.

The rise in insolvencies is mostly IVAs. An IVA, a debt relief order or bankruptcy is a formal step with lasting effects, and free advisers can explain whether any of them is right for someone's situation.
